Context and challenge

Good people spending time on repetitive coordination.

The hypothetical operations team moves information between inboxes, documents, spreadsheets, and internal tools.

The visible problem is duplicated effort. The underlying problems are inconsistent intake, unclear ownership, manual routing, and limited visibility into status.

Research and strategy

Map the work before automating it.

  1. Observe the real workflow

    Document triggers, handoffs, exceptions, tools, decision points, and the workarounds people rely on.

  2. Separate rules from judgement

    Identify deterministic tasks suitable for automation and decisions that require context or human accountability.

  3. Prioritise a useful slice

    Choose a contained workflow where a prototype can demonstrate value without introducing unnecessary operational risk.

  4. Define controls and measures

    Agree review points, fallback behaviour, data handling, and the signals that indicate improvement.

The proposed solution

A connected flow with visible human control.

Structured intake

Relevant information arrives in one consistent format instead of being reconstructed from disconnected messages.

Assisted routing

Rules and AI-supported classification suggest the next step while exceptions remain visible.

Human review

People approve sensitive or uncertain outputs before they move into downstream systems.

Status and learning

A simple operational view highlights progress, exceptions, and opportunities for further refinement.

Validation plan

Measure operational value, not automation theatre.

Time and touchpoints

Compare handling time and manual handoffs against the original workflow baseline.

Accuracy and exceptions

Track review changes, error types, fallback frequency, and the clarity of ownership.

Team confidence

Understand whether people find the workflow easier to follow, control, and improve.
